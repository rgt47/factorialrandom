# Referee Report: Matched-Tuple Randomization for Factorial Clinical Trial Designs
*2026-11-18 16:36 PST*

Manuscript: `analysis/report/report.Rmd` (factorialrandom
workspace, HEAD `dee2dc1`). Target venue assumed: *Statistics
in Medicine*.

## 1. Summary of the manuscript

The manuscript proposes matched-tuple randomization for $2^F$
factorial trials. Subjects drawn from a pre-characterized pool
(a trial-ready cohort) are grouped into tuples of size
$k = 2^F$ by a greedy Mahalanobis heuristic, and the $k$
treatment combinations are permuted uniformly within each
tuple. A pool-oversampling variant forms tuples from a larger
pool and retains the tightest ones. The authors claim four
contributions: tuple-formation algorithms, exact and
asymptotic variance formulas for factorial contrasts, a
simulation study motivated by Alzheimer's disease (AD)
prevention trials, and an R package. The simulation grid (72
scenarios, five methods, 2,000 replicates) is set up, but the
draft discloses that the comparator arms were never produced.
Only the two matched-tuple arms are on disk. An application
section reports a 300-replicate simulation at an
AHEAD-3-45-like design point.

## 2. Overall assessment and recommendation

**Recommendation: reject in current form (encourage
resubmission after substantial work).**

The design idea is sensible and well suited to the trial-ready
cohort setting. However, the manuscript does not yet deliver
any of its stated statistical contributions at a level a
top-tier or specialist journal would accept. The variance
'derivations' are stated without derivation, and the one
closed-form efficiency result appears to be incorrect. The
Results section is built from a simulation file that predates
three correctness fixes, one of which halves every main-effect
estimate; the draft discloses two of these fixes but not the
third. Two of the three comparators are implemented in ways
that do not match their description, so a regenerated
comparison would still not be a fair test. Finally, the
closest prior work, Bai, Liu and Tabord-Meehan (2024) on
inference for matched tuples and fully blocked factorial
designs, is not cited, and it covers most of the
methodological ground the paper claims.

## 3. Significance and novelty

Matched tuples for $k > 2$ arms are not new. Bai, Liu and
Tabord-Meehan (2024, *Quantitative Economics* 15:279-330)
develop estimation and asymptotically exact inference for
matched-tuple designs, explicitly including 'fully blocked'
$2^K$ factorial designs. They show the fully blocked design
dominates stratified factorial designs with finitely many
large strata. That is essentially the paper's Contribution 2
and its theoretical efficiency claim, done rigorously.
Higgins, Sävje and Sekhon (2016, *PNAS* 113:7369-7376) give a
scalable blocking algorithm for multi-arm designs with an
approximation guarantee (threshold blocking), which addresses
Contribution 1 and the 'combinatorially hard' remark.
Rerandomization for $2^K$ factorials is developed by Branson,
Dasgupta and Rubin (2016, *Ann. Appl. Stat.* 10:1958-1976) and
Li, Ding and Rubin (2020, *Ann. Statist.*). None of these
appear in the manuscript.

What remains potentially new is applied: the pool-oversampling
idea in the trial-ready cohort context, and an AD-motivated
evaluation with an R package. That is a reasonable
contribution for *Statistics in Medicine*, *Clinical Trials*,
or *Contemporary Clinical Trials*, provided the simulation is
regenerated, the comparators are fixed, and the theory is
either derived properly or replaced by citation to Bai et al.
The oversampling variant also raises an estimand question
(Major 6) that is the most original part of the paper and
deserves real treatment.

## 4. Major comments

**M1. The Results section is built on a simulation file
generated by a known-incorrect estimator, and the Provenance
note understates this.** [verified] I read
`analysis/data/derived_data/sim_performance.rds` (dated
2026-04-20). For the Neyman estimator with true main effect
0.2, the mean bias is -0.100 and mean coverage is about
0.68. For the interaction with true value 0.15, mean bias is
-0.112 and mean coverage is 0.618. That is, the estimates
recover about half the main effect and a quarter of the
interaction. Over all rows, coverage spans
0.055 to 0.998 and maximum absolute bias is 0.117. These are
the values the inline R code will print in 'Headline
findings'. The cause is the `factorial_contrasts()`
normalization bug (divide by $k$ rather than $k/2$) and the
interaction divisor in `generate_factorial_trial()`, both
fixed on 2026-08-16 according to code comments and
`docs/pub_review_remediation_2026-08-16.md`. The Provenance
paragraph names only the `tuple_balance()` and
`regression_adjustment()` fixes. A reader would conclude that
the matched-tuple numbers on disk are valid and merely lack
comparators. They are not valid. Every figure, the MCSE table,
and the inline ranges in Section 4.2 are artifacts. The
coverage figure also clips at 0.85, which hides most of the
under-coverage. Remedy: rerun `01_run_simulation.R` in full
(parallelized) before any further revision, and in the
interim remove the inline ranges and figures rather than
caveat them.

**M2. The asymptotic relative-efficiency result is not derived
and appears to be wrong.** [inspected; heuristic re-derivation]
Section 3.6.1 states that as $\rho_w \to 1$,
$\text{RE} \to k/(k-1) \cdot M$. No derivation, regularity
conditions, or definition of $\rho_w$ for a contrast is given.
Under the usual model, matching replaces the outcome variance
$\sigma^2$ in each arm-mean variance by roughly
$\sigma^2(1-\rho_w)$. This gives $\text{RE} \approx
1/(1-\rho_w)$, which diverges as $\rho_w \to 1$, or is
bounded by $1/(1-R^2)$ when residual noise is irreducible. A
limit proportional to $M$ (the number of tuples) is
dimensionally implausible, because efficiency relative to
simple randomization should not grow with sample size at
fixed matching quality. The abstract and Discussion both cite
this 'result'. Remedy: derive RE formally under a stated
superpopulation or finite-population model, or replace it with
the asymptotic variance comparison in Bai, Liu and
Tabord-Meehan (2024).

**M3. Contribution 2 (exact and asymptotic variance formulas)
is not delivered.** [inspected] Equation (3) for the
matched-pair variance uses undefined quantities ($\tau_m$ as
$Y_m(1)-Y_m(0)$ at the pair level, and '$\text{Var}_m$'). I
believe, from recall and without re-reading the paper, that it
does not match the finite-population form in Imai (2008).
Section 3.5.2 states $\text{Var}(\hat\tau_c) = M^{-2}\sum_m
V_m(c)$ and says $V_m(c)$ 'depends on the within-tuple
potential outcomes', with no expression. The 'conservative
estimator' is described in one sentence and never shown to be
conservative. The unbiasedness claim in Section 3.5.1 is
asserted without proof. Remedy: give $V_m(c)$ explicitly as a
function of the $k$ units' potential outcomes under the
$k!$ permutation distribution, prove the conservativeness of
the between-tuple variance estimator, and state the
asymptotic distribution. According to its abstract, Bai et al.
(2024) construct a consistent estimator of the asymptotic
variance for matched tuples. The manuscript should engage with
that construction. I have not read the full text, so I cannot
confirm its exact form here.

**M4. The rerandomization comparator is mis-scaled and is
effectively unrestricted randomization.** [verified]
`mahalanobis_balance()` sums pairwise Mahalanobis distances
between arm means using $S^{-1}$, the inverse covariance of
individual covariates, not of mean differences. The threshold
is `qchisq(0.001, df = p*(k-1))`. Because arm-mean
differences shrink as $O(n^{-1/2})$, the statistic shrinks
with $n$ while the threshold is fixed. I drew 400 balanced
allocations per cell: the first-draw acceptance probability
was 0.37 at $(n,p) = (100,3)$ and 1.00 at $(100,8)$,
$(500,3)$ and $(500,8)$. The intended acceptance probability
is 0.001. In the application output, rerandomization has mean
max ASMD 0.273 against 0.280 for simple randomization. The
comparator table also states the threshold as
$\chi^2_{p,0.001}$, which differs from the code's degrees of
freedom, and the Background equation uses the covariance of
the mean difference, which the code does not. Remedy: use the
Morgan-Rubin statistic on the covariance of mean differences
(or the $2^K$ version of Branson et al. 2016), and calibrate
the threshold to a stated acceptance probability.

**M5. The stratified comparator does not match its
description.** [inspected] Table 2 says 'stratify on
top-2-covariate quartiles'. The code stratifies on `X1` only,
using `cut(X1, breaks = 4)`, which gives equal-width
intervals rather than quartiles. With AR(1) covariates and
equal $\beta$ weights there is no 'top-2' ordering. Remedy:
implement what is described, or describe what is implemented.

**M6. Pool oversampling changes the population, and the
estimand is not discussed.** [inspected] With $r > 1$,
`trim_to_best_tuples()` keeps the tuples with smallest mean
within-tuple distance. This systematically excludes subjects
in sparse covariate regions (older, extreme amyloid, rare
genotype). Under a constant effect this is harmless. Under
effect heterogeneity the trial estimates a sample average
effect for a covariate-selected subpopulation, which may
differ from the trial's target population. This is the most
distinctive part of the proposal, and it needs a stated
estimand, a heterogeneous-effect simulation scenario, and a
discussion of eligibility and fairness implications in a
trial-ready cohort. The simulation DGM has constant effects
only, so it cannot reveal this.

**M7. Inference ignores the stated degrees-of-freedom
problem, and the adjusted estimator's SE ignores the fitted
parameters.** [inspected; suspected effect] Section 3.6.2
argues that effective degrees of freedom are $M-1$. All
intervals in `estimate_effects()` nevertheless use $\pm 1.96$
and normal p-values. With $N=100$, $M=25$. The regression
adjustment fits $k(p+1) = 36$ coefficients when $p = 8$ and
then uses unadjusted Neyman residual variances. The on-disk
data cannot test this. Their 'adjusted' rows pair the
unadjusted point estimate with the residual-based SE, because
they predate the `regression_adjustment()` fix (M1). The
concern is therefore suspected, not observed. Remedy: use $t_{M-1}$
for the paired estimator, an HC2-type or Bai et al. variance
for the adjusted one, and report coverage separately by
estimator after the rerun.

**M8. Internal inconsistency on whether a head-to-head
comparison exists.** [verified] The abstract and Section 6.1
say the comparison against simple, stratified and
rerandomization 'is not yet reported'. The Application tables
read `application_example.rds`, which contains all five
methods (300 replicates each, post-fix). Those tables do
report a comparison, at one design point. The authors should
either say so and interpret it with its MCSE, or restrict the
tables.

   *nonbipartite* matching; a single pool is not a bipartite

   limit as 'a truncated normal' is loose. It is a convolution
   of a normal and a truncated normal. [suspected from recall]

   With $\pm 1$ coding and weights $c_1c_2/2$, $\tau_{AB}$ here

12. Discussion 6.2.2: the greedy algorithm is $O(N^2)$ in
    memory and distance computation but $O(N^2 \log N)$ with

13. Internal repository paths (`docs/analysis-plan.md`,
    `docs/pub_review_remediation_2026-08-16.md`), 'TODO (before

## 7. Suggestions for strengthening the paper

1. Regenerate the full sweep with all five methods, after
   fixing M4 and M5, and parallelize `run_condition()`.
2. Add heterogeneous-effect scenarios and non-normal outcomes
   (AD change scores are skewed), and a $2^3$ design ($k=8$)
   to test the degrees-of-freedom concern directly.
3. Add modern comparators: threshold blocking (`quickblock`),
   $2^K$ rerandomization, and Lin-adjusted complete
   randomization. The last is the realistic default in practice.
4. Implement an exact or near-optimal tuple formation (e.g.,
   `nbpMatching` extensions or an ILP for moderate $N$) to
   measure how much the greedy heuristic loses.
5. Report randomization-inference p-values under the
   $(k!)^M$ distribution, which the package can compute cheaply.
6. Treat the application as a design calculation with real
   or realistic TRC-PAD covariate distributions rather than
   AR(1) normal covariates.
